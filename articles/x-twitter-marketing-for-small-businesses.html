X (Twitter) Marketing for Small Businesses: A Practical Guide to Building an Audience and Driving Traffic

X isn't the platform it was a few years ago — the algorithm, the verification system, and the ad economics have all changed — but it's still one of the few places where a small business can get in front of an industry conversation in real time, for free, without a video production budget. Here's what actually works on it now.

A lot of small business marketing advice still treats X the way it treated Twitter in 2018 — post consistently, use hashtags, reply to big accounts, wait for growth. That advice mostly doesn't work anymore. The algorithm now weighs different signals, a large share of reach is sold rather than earned, and the user base has shifted enough that some industries (software, finance, media, politics, sports) still find real customers there while others (local retail, restaurants, most consumer brands) get very little from it at all. This guide covers what's actually changed, who X is still worth the effort for, setup and verification, the content formats and posting habits that get real reach today, how ads and realistic budgets work, X's community features, and the mistakes that waste the most time.

Why X still matters for some small businesses — and not others

X's core value hasn't disappeared: it's still the fastest place online for a real-time, text-first conversation, and it's still where journalists, analysts, and industry insiders in several fields actually spend time looking for sources and opinions. For a B2B software company, a financial advisor, an independent consultant, a media or publishing business, or anyone selling into a professional or technical niche, that's a genuinely useful audience that's hard to reach as directly anywhere else. For a local service business, a restaurant, or most direct-to-consumer retail, the platform's remaining audience skews toward people discussing news, sports, and niche interests rather than local shopping decisions — those businesses usually get a better return from Instagram, Facebook, or TikTok. The honest first question isn't "should we be on X," it's "does our actual customer spend meaningful time reading X," and for a large share of small businesses the answer is genuinely no.

Who X is actually worth the effort for

  • B2B software and SaaS companies, where a founder or product lead sharing genuine insight builds more credibility in a thread than almost any paid channel.
  • Consultants, analysts, and service providers in finance, law, and tech, where being visibly knowledgeable in public conversations drives inbound inquiries.
  • Media, newsletters, and content businesses, since X still drives meaningful click-through traffic for timely, newsworthy content in a way slower-moving platforms don't.
  • Developer tools and technical products, where the platform's remaining concentration of engineers and builders is still dense and responsive.
  • Any business selling to a niche hobbyist or enthusiast community (fitness equipment, trading tools, tabletop gaming, niche software) that still actively congregates on the platform around its interest.

If none of those describe the business, X is still worth claiming as a handle for brand protection and customer service, but it rarely deserves meaningful content production time ahead of other channels.

Setting up and verifying an account correctly

  1. Register as a business, not convert a personal profile — a dedicated account keeps the brand's posting history and analytics clean from day one.
  2. Fill out the full profile: a clear bio stating what the business does and for whom, a link to the website (or a link-in-bio page if there's more than one destination to share), location, and a professional header image.
  3. Decide on X Premium or Verified Organizations deliberately rather than by default — covered in more detail below, since the value depends heavily on how reply-heavy the account's strategy will be.
  4. Set up X Pro (formerly TweetDeck) if more than one person will manage the account, since it makes monitoring mentions, keywords, and multiple lists far more practical than the standard app.
  5. Turn on two-factor authentication immediately — business accounts with any following are a common account-takeover target, and recovering a hijacked account is slow.

Content formats that actually get reach now

The accounts still growing on X share a few habits in common, regardless of industry:

  • Threads that teach something specific — a numbered breakdown of how to do one narrow thing well outperforms a single vague tip, and threads keep people reading (and signal engagement) longer than a one-line post.
  • Original screenshots and data — a chart, a before/after, a real product screenshot, or a genuine result shared transparently earns more engagement than stock imagery or generic commentary.
  • Replying substantively inside relevant conversations, not just posting from the brand's own timeline — a thoughtful reply under a larger account's popular post reaches people who would never see a standalone brand post.
  • Native video and images, which the platform still visibly favors in distribution over a bare link, since an external link historically reduced reach (a long-running pattern that favors keeping the link in the first reply or bio instead of the main post).
  • Polls, which are cheap to produce and reliably pull in replies and quote-posts from an existing audience, helping a post's early engagement signal before the algorithm decides how far to push it.

How the For You algorithm actually ranks a post

X has published parts of its recommendation system's workings, and the behavior lines up with what most active accounts observe: early engagement velocity (replies and reposts especially, more than likes) in the first 15–30 minutes after posting heavily predicts how far a post travels, verified or Premium-subscriber accounts get a documented boost in reply and reach weighting, and posts that keep people on the platform (no outbound link, or a link placed outside the main post text) tend to get wider initial distribution than ones that send readers elsewhere immediately. None of that means an unverified free account can't get real reach — strong engagement still carries a post regardless of subscription status — but it does mean the deck is measurably stacked toward paying accounts and toward content that keeps the conversation happening in-app.

The clearest return shows up for accounts that are genuinely reply-heavy — a support team, a founder answering questions publicly, a brand that lives in the replies of its own industry's conversations. An account that mostly broadcasts without engaging gets much less value from the subscription, since the main benefit (reply visibility and ranking) only compounds with actual conversation.

X Ads and realistic costs

X's ad platform runs on an auction, and a small business typically works with a narrow set of formats:

FormatWhat it doesBest use
Promoted PostsBoosts a normal post into more feedsAmplifying a post that's already performing organically
Follower campaignsOptimizes toward new followersEarly account growth within a defined interest audience
Website/App campaignsOptimizes toward clicks or conversions off-platformDriving traffic to a landing page, signup, or product
Amplify / video pre-rollPlaces a video ad against publisher video contentBrands with produced video and a larger budget

Costs vary heavily by industry and audience competitiveness, so treat any number as a rough planning range rather than a guarantee: a small test budget run over two to three weeks, comparing two or three creative variations against a tightly defined interest or keyword audience, is the most reliable way to find a business's actual cost-per-click before committing a larger monthly spend. Boosting a post that's already earning organic replies and reposts tends to outperform a cold-started ad built with no engagement history behind it, the same pattern that holds on most social platforms.

Communities, Spaces, and building an actual audience

Two underused features consistently help a small account grow faster than relying on the main timeline alone. Communities — topic-specific groups inside X — let a brand post into an already-interested audience that doesn't follow the account yet, which is a meaningfully easier way to get in front of new people than hoping a cold post reaches them organically. Spaces — live audio rooms — work well for a founder or specialist willing to host a recurring, informal conversation; they rarely need heavy production, and a consistent weekly or biweekly Space builds the kind of direct audience familiarity that a text feed alone doesn't. Neither feature replaces a core content strategy, but both are close to free distribution channels that most competitors in a given niche still aren't using.

What to actually measure

X's native analytics (available to any account, with more detail for Premium subscribers) surface impressions, engagement rate, profile visits, and link clicks per post — the numbers worth a weekly check are reply rate specifically (the strongest predictor of future algorithmic reach), profile-visit-to-follow conversion (whether the content that reaches new people actually convinces them to follow), and, for accounts running ads, cost per click or cost per follower against the campaign's actual goal. Raw impression counts are the weakest signal to optimize toward — a post can rack up impressions from being shown broadly without driving any of the replies or clicks that actually matter to the business.

Common mistakes

  • Posting only broadcast-style announcements and never replying inside other conversations, missing most of the platform's actual distribution mechanism.
  • Putting the link in the main post text on every post instead of the first reply or bio, quietly suppressing reach on content meant to drive traffic.
  • Treating X like Instagram or TikTok with heavily produced, caption-light visuals instead of the text-and-conversation format the platform actually rewards.
  • Buying Premium for the checkmark alone without changing posting habits to actually use the reply-ranking boost it provides.
  • Ignoring Communities and Spaces entirely and relying only on the main feed, leaving free distribution unused.
  • Running ads with no organic engagement history instead of testing content organically first and boosting what already works.
  • Staying on the platform by habit when the actual target customer genuinely isn't there, instead of reallocating that time to a channel that is.

Conclusion

X today rewards a narrower, more conversation-driven strategy than the "post consistently and watch it grow" advice that circulated years ago — early engagement velocity, genuine replies inside other people's conversations, and content that keeps people in-app now matter more than raw posting frequency. For businesses whose actual customers spend time there — B2B, technical, financial, and niche enthusiast audiences especially — it's still one of the more direct, low-cost ways to build credibility and drive real traffic. For businesses whose customers don't, the better use of that time is almost always a different channel.
