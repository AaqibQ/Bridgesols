Digital Marketing Benchmarks for US Small Businesses in 2026

What "good" actually looks like — realistic ad costs, conversion rates, and channel performance, so you can judge your own numbers against something real instead of guessing.

"Is this good?" is the question every small business owner asks after looking at their ad dashboard, and it's genuinely hard to answer without a reference point. Benchmarks vary meaningfully by industry and region, so treat the ranges below as a sanity check, not a guarantee — but they're a far better starting point than comparing yourself to a case study from a company ten times your size.

Why benchmarks vary so much by industry

A "good" cost-per-click in legal services ($6–$10+) looks nothing like a good CPC in retail e-commerce ($0.50–$1.50). Before applying any benchmark below, identify your actual category — comparing a local service business's numbers to a national e-commerce brand's numbers will produce a misleading conclusion either way.

Paid search (Google Ads) benchmarks

MetricTypical range (small business)
Average CPC$1 – $4 (many service categories run higher, $6–$15+)
Average click-through rate3% – 6% for well-matched ad copy
Average conversion rate3% – 8%, varies heavily by offer and landing page quality
Average cost per lead$30 – $80 for local services; often higher for competitive B2B categories

Paid social (Meta Ads) benchmarks

MetricTypical range (small business)
Average CPC$0.50 – $2
Average CPM$8 – $16
Average conversion rate (e-commerce)1.5% – 4%
Average cost per lead (service business)$15 – $50

If your numbers sit meaningfully outside these ranges in either direction, it's worth investigating specifically — unusually low costs can signal a narrow, low-competition niche (good) or poor targeting reaching an unqualified audience (bad); unusually high costs often point to weak ad creative or an overly broad audience.

Organic and SEO benchmarks

  • Time to meaningful ranking movement: 3–6 months for a new page targeting a moderately competitive keyword, assuming consistent publishing and basic technical SEO health.
  • Organic click-through rate for a #1 ranking: roughly 25–35% of searches for that query, dropping sharply by position 3–5.
  • Blog-driven lead conversion rate: typically lower than paid-intent traffic (0.5%–2%) but at near-zero marginal cost once published, and compounding over time — see our SEO for Beginners guide.

Email marketing benchmarks

MetricTypical range
Average open rate20% – 35%, depending on list quality and send frequency
Average click-through rate2% – 5%
Unsubscribe rate (healthy)Under 0.5% per send

A smaller, genuinely opted-in list with 30%+ open rates is worth more than a large, purchased or scraped list with single-digit engagement — engagement rate, not list size, is what most email platforms and inbox providers weight when deciding whether your future emails land in the inbox at all.

A note on regional and regulatory differences

Marketing compliance in the US isn't fully uniform across states — data privacy rules (like California's CCPA) and industry-specific advertising regulations (financial services, healthcare, legal) can affect what claims you can make and what data you can collect, depending on where your customers are located. This isn't legal advice, and requirements change, but it's worth a specific compliance check if you operate in a regulated category or collect meaningful customer data, rather than assuming one national standard applies everywhere.

Using these numbers without misleading yourself

  1. Compare against your own category and business size, not the broadest available average.
  2. Track your own numbers over time — a benchmark tells you roughly where you stand today, but your own trend line tells you whether you're improving.
  3. Treat a benchmark as a diagnostic starting point, not a target to hit for its own sake — a lower conversion rate with a higher average order value can still be the better outcome.

Conclusion

Benchmarks are most useful as a reality check, not a scoreboard. Know your category before comparing your numbers to anyone else's, watch your own trend over time more closely than any single external number, and remember that a metric only matters to the extent it's connected to actual leads, sales, or profit.
