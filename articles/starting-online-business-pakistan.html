Starting an Online Business in Pakistan: Registration, Taxes, and Realistic Costs in 2026

The paperwork questions that actually matter before you take your first order — sole proprietorship vs. company registration, SECP and FBR, sales tax on services, and which payment setup actually works.

Most advice on starting an online business in Pakistan jumps straight to "pick a niche, build a Shopify store, run Facebook ads." That skips the part that's actually hard to undo once you're mid-way through: how you're registered, whether you need SECP at all, and how you're going to legally receive payment from a buyer who isn't in Pakistan. None of this is exciting, but getting it wrong costs real time later — a frozen payment gateway account, a tax notice for income nobody declared, or a bank that won't open a current account without paperwork you didn't know you needed. This guide covers that groundwork, not legal or tax advice, so you know what to ask an accountant, a tax consultant, or SECP's own eServices helpdesk.

Do you actually need to register a company?

Not immediately, and not always. If you're testing a product on Instagram with a handful of orders a month, you can legally operate as an unregistered individual and declare that income on your personal tax return once you start filing. Registration starts to matter once any of these become true: you want a business bank account instead of a personal one receiving customer payments, you need an NTN under a business name to issue proper invoices to B2B clients, you want to apply for a payment gateway or import a Google/Meta ad account under a business, or your revenue has grown past the point where mixing it with personal transactions makes bookkeeping and tax filing a mess.

Choosing a business structure

Sole proprietorship: the simplest starting point

A sole proprietorship isn't registered with SECP at all — it's just you, trading under your own name or a business name, with an NTN from the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR) and, if you want one, a business bank account opened against that NTN and a letterhead/invoice showing the business name. It's the fastest and cheapest way to start selling legally, and it's what most small online sellers and freelancers in Pakistan actually use in year one. The tradeoff is the same as anywhere: no legal separation between you and the business, so business debts or disputes are personal liabilities too.

Single Member Company and Private Limited: when to incorporate

Once liability or credibility starts to matter — you're taking on a co-founder, signing contracts with larger clients, applying for a payment gateway that requires a registered entity, or raising investment — incorporating with SECP is the next step. Two structures fit most online businesses:

  • Single Member Company (SMC-Private Limited) — a limited liability company with one owner. It gives the liability protection of a company without needing a second shareholder, and it's become the default choice for solo founders who've outgrown a sole proprietorship.
  • Private Limited Company — requires at least two shareholders and a nominated company secretary in most cases, and is the standard structure once there's more than one founder or outside investment involved.

Both are registered through SECP's e-Services portal, and both separate personal and business liability the way an LLC does in other markets — SECP's filing fees and the registration process are comparable whichever you pick, so the deciding factor is usually ownership structure (one founder vs. more than one), not cost.

Registering a company with SECP

  1. Reserve a company name through SECP's e-Services portal — check availability first; a name too close to an existing registered company is rejected.
  2. Prepare the Memorandum and Articles of Association — SECP provides standard templates for simple single-owner or two-shareholder companies, so a lawyer isn't strictly required for a straightforward setup, though one is worth it if ownership terms are more than boilerplate.
  3. Submit incorporation documents online through e-Services, along with copies of CNICs for all directors/shareholders.
  4. Pay the SECP filing fee, which scales with authorized capital — for a small SMC or two-shareholder private limited with modest authorized capital, this is typically in the low tens of thousands of rupees.
  5. Receive your Certificate of Incorporation, which is required before you can open a company bank account or apply for most payment gateways under the business name.
  6. Register for an NTN for the company (covered below) once incorporated.

Getting an NTN and registering with FBR

A National Tax Number is required whether you're a sole proprietor or a registered company — it's what lets you file income tax returns, issue tax invoices, and in most cases open a business bank account. Apply through FBR's IRIS portal; registration itself is free. For a sole proprietorship, you register using your own CNIC; for an SMC or Private Limited, the company registers separately under its SECP incorporation details. Once registered, you're expected to file an annual income tax return — even in a year with modest or no profit, filing (rather than skipping) is what keeps you off FBR's non-filer list, which carries higher withholding tax rates on banking transactions and other costs that make non-filing more expensive than it looks.

Opening a business bank account

Keep business and personal money separate from the first rupee, for the same reason it matters anywhere: mixing funds makes bookkeeping unreliable and, for a registered company, risks undermining the liability protection incorporation is supposed to provide. Banks typically require your NTN certificate, and for a company, the SECP Certificate of Incorporation plus a board resolution naming the account signatories. Several Pakistani banks now offer dedicated digital/business account tiers aimed at small online sellers and freelancers, with lower minimum balance requirements than a traditional corporate account — worth comparing before defaulting to whichever bank you already use personally.

Sales tax: services vs. goods, and why the rules differ by province

Sales tax in Pakistan splits in a way that trips up a lot of new online sellers:

  • Goods — sales tax on physical goods is federal, administered by FBR, with registration required once turnover crosses the threshold set in each year's Finance Act (thresholds and rates change periodically, so verify the current figure rather than relying on last year's number).
  • Services — sales tax on services is provincial, not federal. If you're selling a service (design, development, consulting, digital marketing) rather than a physical product, you register with your province's revenue authority — the Sindh Revenue Board (SRB), Punjab Revenue Authority (PRA), KPRA in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, or BRA in Balochistan — not with FBR directly for this specific tax, even though income tax still goes through FBR.

Many small online sellers and solo service providers stay under the registration threshold in their first year, which is part of why a sole proprietorship with simple FBR income tax filing is enough to start — but check the current threshold for your category rather than assuming you're exempt once revenue grows past a modest level.

Payment gateways and getting paid, including from abroad

This is where a lot of otherwise well-planned Pakistani online businesses hit friction, because the big international processors (Stripe, PayPal's full merchant features) aren't fully available the way they are in the US or UAE. Here's the realistic landscape:

Realistic startup costs

"Starting an online business costs nothing" undersells the paperwork, even in a market with genuinely low entry costs compared to the US or UAE. A more honest first-year range:

ItemTypical range (PKR)
NTN registration (FBR)Free (self-filed) to a few thousand if using a tax consultant
SECP incorporation (SMC or Private Limited, if applicable)Roughly 15,000–40,000, depending on authorized capital and whether you use a lawyer/consultant
Domain name1,500–4,000/year
Website platform or hosting3,000–15,000/month, depending on platform and traffic
Business bank accountOften free to open; minimum balance requirements vary by bank and account tier
Bookkeeping (basic software or a part-time accountant)3,000–15,000/month
Annual income tax filing (if outsourced)5,000–25,000/year for a straightforward sole proprietorship or SMC

None of this includes ad spend, inventory, or your own time — but it's a realistic floor for the legal and operational basics before marketing even starts.

A note on e-commerce-specific rules

Pakistan's regulatory framework for e-commerce has been tightening — expect requirements around displaying clear return/refund policies, accurate product descriptions, and (for larger sellers) sales tax registration on online marketplace transactions specifically. If you're selling through a marketplace like Daraz rather than your own site, the marketplace typically handles some of this at the platform level, but you're still responsible for your own tax filing and for honest product listings regardless of which channel you sell through.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Incorporating with SECP before you need to — a sole proprietorship with an NTN is enough for most sellers in year one, and incorporation adds filing obligations you don't yet need.
  • Mixing personal and business bank accounts, which makes tax filing harder and can undermine liability protection once you do incorporate.
  • Assuming service sales tax works like goods sales tax — registering with FBR for goods when your actual obligation (if any) is with a provincial revenue authority for services, or vice versa.
  • Becoming an FBR non-filer by skipping annual returns, which costs more in higher withholding tax than filing would have.
  • Over-relying on PayPal for international client payments without a backup (Payoneer, bank wire, or a local gateway), given PayPal's limited merchant functionality in Pakistan.
  • Underestimating COD return rates when planning e-commerce logistics and cash flow.

Conclusion

None of this is the part that feels like "starting a business," which is exactly why it gets skipped — until a bank asks for an NTN you don't have, or a payment gateway application stalls because there's no SECP certificate behind it. Start as a sole proprietorship if that's genuinely enough for what you're doing, register with FBR either way, and only incorporate with SECP once liability, credibility, or a co-founder makes it worth the added filing. Get the payment setup sorted before launch, not after your first order arrives with nowhere legitimate for the money to land.
