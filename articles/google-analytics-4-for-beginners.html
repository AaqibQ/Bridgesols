Google Analytics 4 for Beginners: The Numbers That Actually Matter

GA4 can show hundreds of numbers. Only a few of them change what you do next — here's how to find them and how to read them without fooling yourself.

BE BridgeSols Editorial · Published September 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) is free, powerful, and famously confusing to open for the first time. The good news is that you don't need most of it. A small site can learn nearly everything it needs from five or six numbers, checked on a regular schedule. This guide covers what GA4 measures, how to set it up correctly, and which reports are worth your attention.

How GA4 works, in plain terms

Older versions of Analytics were built around page views and sessions. GA4 is built around events — individual actions such as loading a page, scrolling, clicking a link, or submitting a form. Some events are collected automatically; others you define yourself. Every report is a different way of grouping and counting those events, which is why the same data can look different depending on where you view it.

Setting it up correctly

  1. Create a GA4 property in your Google Analytics account and add a web data stream for your domain.
  2. Install the tag once. Copy the Measurement ID (it starts with "G-") and add the Google tag to every page. Adding it twice inflates your numbers.
  3. Verify with the Realtime report. Open your own site in another tab and confirm you appear within a minute.
  4. Link Google Search Console in the property's admin settings so you can see which search queries bring visitors.
  5. Exclude your own visits where possible, so your testing doesn't distort a small site's numbers.

The numbers that matter

MetricThe question it answers
Users (and new users)How many different people are visiting, and how many are new?
Traffic by channelWhere do visitors come from — search, social, direct, referrals?
Engagement rateDo people actually interact with the page or leave immediately?
Top landing pagesWhich pages are the front door to the site?
Key eventsHow many visitors take an action that matters to the business?

Everything else is context. If a number doesn't lead to a decision, it's a distraction.

Track the actions that matter

GA4 calls important actions key events (previously "conversions"). For a content or services site, sensible examples include a contact form submission, a click on a WhatsApp or email link, or a newsletter signup. Decide what a valuable visit looks like for your business, mark those events as key events, and track how many happen each week. Page views alone don't tell you whether the site is working — actions do.

Reading small numbers honestly

A new site has small numbers, and small numbers are noisy. Ten visitors instead of five is a 100% jump that means almost nothing. A few habits keep you from drawing wrong conclusions:

  • Compare weeks, not days, and look at trends over several weeks.
  • Don't change strategy based on one unusual day.
  • Remember that your own visits, bots, and testing can make up a large share of a tiny total.
  • Treat search-engine traffic as slow to build — new content often takes weeks or months to gain visibility.

Privacy and consent

GA4 uses cookies and identifiers to recognise returning visitors. Depending on where your visitors live — the European Economic Area and the UK in particular — you may need their consent before analytics or advertising cookies load. Your privacy policy should describe what you collect and why. Rules differ by country and change over time, so check current requirements for the regions you serve. This is general information, not legal advice.

A 15-minute weekly review

  1. Open the traffic-by-channel report and note the week's total users compared with the previous week.
  2. Check the top landing pages — is new content starting to appear?
  3. Look at engagement rate on your main pages. A very low number on an important page is worth investigating.
  4. Count key events this week. Write the number down.
  5. Open Search Console and note any new queries you're appearing for.
  6. Decide on one small action for next week — update a page, write a related article, or fix a broken link.

Keeping a simple log of these numbers turns analytics from a dashboard you glance at into a record of what's working. Our guide on how to grow a website covers what to do with those insights.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Installing the tag twice, which doubles counts.
  • Tracking everything and reviewing nothing. A few metrics reviewed weekly beat fifty metrics ignored.
  • Judging content by page views alone instead of actions and engagement.
  • Forgetting consent and privacy disclosure for visitors in regulated regions.

Conclusion

GA4 becomes manageable once you decide what you actually need to know: where visitors come from, what they read, and whether they take the actions that matter. Set it up once and correctly, track a handful of key events, review the same few numbers each week, and be honest about how little a small sample can tell you.
